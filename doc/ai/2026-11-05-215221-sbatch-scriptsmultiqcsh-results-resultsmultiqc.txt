❯ could you explain why the adapter length distribution graph has the most
  common adapter lengths being 1-5BP

● That's expected and not a red flag — it's just how FastQC's
  adapter-content/length detection works, not a biological phenomenon.

  FastQC (and MultiQC's rendering of it) reports adapter contamination by
  counting, at each read position, how many reads have a short k-mer match to a
  known adapter sequence (default k=10-ish, but it reports matches starting at
  very short overlap lengths). When trimming/detection tools scan for adapter
  matches, short partial matches (1-5bp) at read ends are vastly more common
  than long matches purely by chance — with only 4 possible bases, a 1-5bp
  "match" to an adapter sequence happens randomly very frequently just by base
  composition, not because real adapter is there. Longer matches (10bp+) are
  statistically much rarer to occur randomly, so they're much less frequent and
  are the ones that actually indicate real adapter read-through.

  So the graph's peak at 1-5bp is dominated by random/spurious short matches,
  not genuine adapter contamination. What you should actually look at is whether
  there's a meaningful tail of longer-length matches (e.g., 20bp+) — that's the
  real signal of adapter contamination needing trimming. If the curve drops off
  sharply after ~5-10bp, your data is likely fine.
